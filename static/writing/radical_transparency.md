the liberation comes not only from a lack of fear, but a lack of persistent thought at all. attention is a resource just as important as time, and giving little things such as "how should i phrase this? should i care?"

the biggest thing i got liberation from was understanding that my entire life i was overwhelmingly performative. before high school, i switched between 7 schools across china, michigan and california. in high school, i have been dabbling in various very different groups in tech and sports. i learned to adapt and make friends, and i think a maladaption was to be highly performative. all of this interacting with older, more experienced folks or even just operating in unfamiliar environments i have built an unconscious impulsive instinct to impress and shapeshift into the most likable form[^14]. this is great and all but recently i have realized that not doing it allows me to feel so much better during a conversation. its like having my muscles tensed for my entire life and only now experiencing what it is like to untense them. 

a couple weeks ago when speaking with a close friend, i for the first time did all of the following—i
- actually went to the bathroom when i needed to (before i would hold it)
- drank water
- didnt follow what they get from a restaurant blindly
- was honest and talked about things im deeply insecure about
- breathed in deeply when i felt like it
- walked around / enjoyed the sun
- was not tense and hyper conscious & noticing of every detail
- was not constantly thinking about what i could have done better
- i did not nod along to everything they said and instead actually thought about it
- i did not retreat at the slightest presence of any criticism
bits and pieces were there before but not all at once.

---

> "The freedom to be fully honest with other people is hard to overrate or even describe. It is always available to you."[^8]

after thinking about it for a good amount, i think i have figured out why doing so feels so good. its because i was in constant psychological pain that i didnt even realize because i never had a baseline. its insanely scary to keep it all inside and feel terrified that droplets of it will leak from my brain through your unconscious and eventually the dam will break and the sky will fall; and insanely liberating to feel this fear melt away. this, by the way, is the psychological pain that [has been shown (somewhat tenuously) to light up the same brain regions as physical pain](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/9056800_Does_Rejection_Hurt_An_fMRI_Study_of_Social_Exclusion). it was caused by internal conflict between who i was and who in that moment i wanted to be. one side pulled towards the ground, one side pulled towards the sky where possibilities were endless.[^15]

i used to use granola / voice memos as much as possible due to wanting to keep track of everything, and would even make shortcuts on my phone to use it in person. i always felt squirmish when doing this; on my phone and computer the granola logo would be in the dynamic island, lurking.[^6] i squirmed. in that moment, i was a person who was not concerned for other's privacy, but really flipping wanted to be someone who did. i have started asking people if i can record or not or just straight up not recording to be more self consistent.

when i introduce myself with awards or credentials to be impressive but i dont actually feel are a part of my identity, i feel this kind of pain as well. instead, i talk about the things i care a lot about / other interesting weird thoughts. ive stopped asking people what they do when i meet them, instead i ask what they care most about.

---
one of the reasons i think people have reacted so kindly to my many doohickeys and mishaps and feedbacks is that im genuine about it. i think people like honesty. not enough people in the world these days are unfiltered. notably for a moment fear, uncertainty, and the game of competition <-> "im better / more classy" are not really a thing anymore. (i cannot read minds so i dont know exactly how others have reacted to my doohickeys and mishaps and feedbacks however it seems on the surface that it has been taken well through observation of response and sustained interaction)

*no one is perfect and its easy to forget that in a world of perfectionism; being vulnerable makes it feel emotionally true*

by being vulnerable im showing i trust you. something something fear is the mind killer and BAM all of a sudden [the grey coats are coming off](https://arc.net/l/quote/wurtnusm) and we are having a good time. i find this strategy to be a very strong way to connect with people; i talk more about specific strategies that have been ingrained into my brain [here](https://harrisonqian.com/writing/on_connection#softening-the-shell-putting-the-gray-coat-on-the-coat-hanger). i like to share embarrassing stories, weird funny experiences, personal mindsets, writing, projects, products im enjoying, raw life updates[^12].

its a really fun time when both people are thinking like this! recently i was on a walk with a cofounder of the company im working at and on it he asked me for feedback on the company's culture and operations. after answering, i asked him for feedback on me. he then asked me for more feedback. i then asked him for more feedback.

"why arent more people doing this?" you may be asking. yeah i wonder this too. it could be that people are not very reflective. it could be that people are not confident. it could be that people are doing it but its hard to tell (think back to that one unhinged friend). it could be that people have been burned too many times and have learned a filter. it could be that people dont care about improving (i think this is very common and in no way a wrong take). there are many possible reasons and i dont know that much about the world. 

**notably it isnt trivial to implement**. it was not trivial for me to implement. the pull towards acting like sheep in today's society is strong[^13]! i have felt strong positive reinforcement from agreement and similarity, lack of self identity, and the fear of being ostracized / made fun of. discussed somewhat in [everything popular is wrong](https://harrisonqian.com/writing/everything_popular_is_wrong). like many things[^1], its a simple principle that is non trivial to implement and goes a long way. 

---
there are always mishaps!

a couple of days ago N gave me a piece of feedback which is that he noticed that i assign value to people based on how thoughtful/introspective they are
as we were talking about it, i mostly agreed with him, but felt like he wasn't really listening to me
he is one year older than me, and in the moment i felt like he felt like he was wiser or somehow more experienced than me, and was giving me advice from a pedestal
i became upset with him for a second but then it dissipated. and then afterwards we talked about it.

at times i receive feedback and feel misinterpreted; i become defensive
at times im hurt by what others say, especially if it rings true with an insecurity
at times i perceive people as judgy, stingy, pretentious
**i can only imagine that others will feel the same way at least sometimes.** 
communication is almost always the solution!

---

to close, i want to say that being honest has been a nice, modular principle. it is surprisingly easy to apply to dilemmas and ive found it generally nudges situations towards positive outcomes. it is one of the first layer principles i use to explore and organize strategy space as i am figuring out things about living life right now. 

"admit to failing?" yes
- a) maybe what i think is failing is actually normal or good; i wouldnt know until i said something
- b) maybe i am failing so much that i shouldn’t be doing the thing and stopping would be good for me
- c) maybe even if i am failing teammates are completely ok with me working with them bc i learn to become faster
- d) i can learn so much by reporting confidences and talking with ppl about it

like all principles, there are intuitive exceptions:
- i do not say every single piece of feedback to every single person
- i still try to impress certain people and am mindful of my first impression especially with ppl who i know nothing about
	- although i would like to think that now when i do it it is not unconscious and i can control how much misalignment i cause for a goal i have
- lying about my age for convenience (which i wont have to do soon!!)
- adding hedging phrases such as "sorry for unsolicited feedback" or "just my 2 cents :)" when giving feedback instead of just giving it raw

i recommend people give it a try and see what its like.
